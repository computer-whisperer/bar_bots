# Target design: routing for honest accounting — travel time over terrain and threat

Written 2026-09-20, late, at the user's direction after the micro lane (`2026-09-20-micro-lane.md`). The user
sampled the lane's batch and still saw "ridiculous movement selections"; the ruling: "we should be able to generate a
decent pathfind loop ourself to accurately price movement. Paths through enemy territory deserve appropriate
handling, and casual commander deployments across the map to reach a spot nearby in space should be priced
honestly." And on execution: no thousand-waypoint routes where the engine's own routing will do; "the internal
coarse estimation should be for our own honest accounting, not actual execution. The only point where we may need a
waypoint should be for deliberate enemy avoidance."

## What is true today (micro-ab2 records, lane-on arm, by minute 10)
- The commander walks 6,500-12,400 elmos a game in the first ten minutes (37.5 elmos a second: three to five and a
  half minutes of the ten), in 5-12 trips over 400 elmos, the longest 1,000-3,200. Most of our build power in the
  opening is on foot.
- `crates/terrain` has one kind of field: Dijkstra over the slope map's 16-elmo cells, uniform cost per passable cell
  (no slope, no threat), for one movement class (the raider's, `BOT2`), 8-connected, corners not cut. Fields exist
  from home, from the enemy bases, and (since 840a168) one per metal spot; 10 ms each on Quicksilver.
- Every "how far" is one of: a straight line (most), a field distance (spot claims, scout routes, raid candidates,
  the leash), or a straight segment tested against turret reach (the raid's approach). Nothing is a time. Nothing
  knows that the commander (`COMMANDERBOT`) and vehicles (`TANK2`) cross different ground from bots.
- Orders are single Move or Fight points; the engine routes. A party sent past a base walks through it unless the
  segment test caught a known turret.
- The engine's speed law on a slope is `1 / (1 + slope * slopeMod)` (`GroundMoveMath.cpp`), `slope` the slope map's
  value and `slopeMod` per movement class (`UnitDef_MoveData_getSlopeMod`); the shim does not send `slopeMod`.
- Threat memory exists twice: the territory grid (`territory.rs`, 256-elmo cells: whose ground, what force can
  arrive in 20 s, what was lost there in three minutes) and the lane's per-tick grid (16-elmo cells, damage a second
  from what is in sight and remembered).

## 1. Weighted fields per movement class (`crates/terrain`)
- A field's cost is **effective elmos**: each cell step costs its length divided by the class's speed modifier on
  that cell's slope, plus a threat penalty in elmos. Travel time is effective elmos over the unit's speed, so one
  field serves every unit of a class.
- One passable mask and one slope-modifier table per class we field: bots, the commander, vehicles (hover and ships
  when a game has them). `MoveClass` gains `slope_mod`; the shim sends it.
- Two flavours per destination: **plain** (terrain only: how long the walk is) and **safe** (terrain plus threat:
  how long the walk is round what we know to be dangerous). A route is read off a field by descending its gradient
  from the unit; no search per query. The route's own reach test replaces the straight segment.
- The threat penalty for the safe flavour, per cell: a wall (impassable) inside the known reach of every remembered
  armed building and the commander's ground; a multiplier on cells the territory grid calls theirs or contested,
  by its threat there. Rebuilt on the worker thread when the set of known turrets or the territory changes, at most
  every few seconds; the plain flavour once.
- Cost: a field is 10 ms; the commander's, the constructors' and the party's destinations are a handful at a time.
  Fields from every metal spot stay (44 plain ones; safe ones only for the spots in play).

## 2. Honest commander pricing (`economy.rs`)
- A commander trip is priced as **build seconds lost**: the round-trip travel time on the commander's safe field
  times its build power, in the opening search's units. It goes when what the destination is worth (the
  extractor's income until a constructor could have got there, the search's own value of the step) exceeds that,
  or when nothing else can do it. The leash becomes a time (seconds of walking), not a radius.
- Constructors are priced the same way against each other: the spot goes to the builder whose travel time is least,
  not whose straight line is.
- Assumption stated: the user has not ruled on the price form explicitly; "priced honestly" is taken to mean travel
  time times build power against the step's worth. It will keep the commander at home far more, with constructors
  carrying expansion, which is how the players play. If the opening's feel suffers (the searched opening's early
  extractors are the commander's), the search's own plan is the exception: its steps are pre-priced.

## 3. Where routes replace lines, in order
1. The spot claim, the leash and the constructors' choice: travel time on the plain field of each spot (today: field
   distance from the spot, straight line for the leash).
2. The raid's approach and alternatives: the route on the party's safe field must exist (a wall means no way in
   without crossing known reach); its plain-field reach test replaces `to_segment`. A party sent elsewhere gets the
   route's first turning as one waypoint only when the safe route and the engine's route (the plain one) part.
3. Contact answers: who arrives first by travel time on a field from the party's place (one field per priced party,
   on the thread; straight lines until it arrives).
4. Scouts' routes and posts: by travel time; a scout's route avoids known reach (safe flavour).
5. Retreats (the commander's, the lane's release, a party going home): the safe route's first turning as the
   waypoint, the destination queued after it. This is the "deliberate enemy avoidance" case and the only one.

## 4. Judged by
- Commander elmos walked by 10:00 and trips over 400 (today 6,500-12,400 and 5-12), from the records; extractors
  by 3:00 and 5:00 unchanged (the opening must not slow).
- Parties that crossed known reach on the way to a target (records: a raid member inside a remembered turret's
  reach before its target), today unmeasured; `run/micro_ledger.py` gains the count.
- Army order changes within ten seconds (today hundreds a game, the lane's steps included).
- The exchange and wins as always. A/B by `--ab-disable` of the pricing rule.

## Order of work
1. `crates/terrain`: slope-weighted fields per class, the safe flavour with a threat layer given by the caller,
   gradient routes with a reach test; tests. `MoveClass.slope_mod` in the protocol and the shim.
2. `routes.rs`: fields per class, safe flavours for destinations in play on the worker thread; `travel_seconds`,
   `route`, `route_crosses`.
3. The commander's price and the leash in time; constructors' choice by time. One instrumented game; a batch.
4. The raid's approach and alternatives by route; contact answers by arrival time; scouts.
5. Retreat waypoints. Each step a commit with its rows.
